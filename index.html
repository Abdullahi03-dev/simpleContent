The strategy

Two platforms. Two posts a day. One goal: paying users.

You don't need followers — you need people who have an interview coming and are scared of failing it. That's the only human who pays for a Sprint. So the question isn't "where's the biggest audience," it's "where do people announce they're job-hunting."

The decision

Go deep on Twitter + LinkedIn

Skip the video platforms for now. Reels and TikTok are reach machines, not intent machines — a person scrolling entertainment isn't reaching for their wallet. Do two platforms properly instead of five badly.

LinkedIn

People literally post "open to work," "got an interview," "just got laid off." That is someone reaching for their wallet. The highest-intent platform that exists for interview prep — and you're barely using it.

Why two a day

One post can't do two jobs

Value in the morning, spark in the afternoon

A deep thread that proves you know the market is a different tool from a poll or hot take that starts conversations and surfaces buyers. Cram both into one post and each one is weaker. Split them: the ☀️ morning post builds authority; the 🌙 afternoon post creates the reply/DM where a follower quietly becomes a signup. Two focused shots beat one blurry one — and it doubles your chances of the algorithm picking you up that day.

Why not video

You confirmed it — here's the reasoning

Reach ≠ revenue

A viral Reel gets 100k views from people who will never interview this year. One LinkedIn comment on an "I have an interview Friday" post reaches one person who might pay you today. As a solo founder chasing your first paying users, you buy intent, not views. Video comes later, once you have wins to show and time to spare.
